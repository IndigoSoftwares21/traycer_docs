---
title: "Rotate signing secret"
description: "Issue a new webhook signing secret. Shown once, and the old one dies immediately."
openapi: "POST /webhooks/subscriptions/{subscriptionId}/rotate-secret"
---

<Note>
  Requires a `read_write` scope key. Takes no request body.
</Note>

Generates a new signing secret for the subscription and returns it, prefixed `whsec_`.

<Warning>
  **Two things happen at once, and both are irreversible:**

  1. This response is the **only** place the new secret is ever shown. No endpoint returns it afterwards.
  2. The old secret stops working **immediately**. There is no overlap window where both verify.

  Between this call and deploying the new value, every delivery fails your signature check — and failed deliveries are not retried. Those events are lost.
</Warning>

## Rotate without losing events

If you cannot deploy the new secret within seconds, do not rotate in place. Run two subscriptions instead:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Register a second URL">
    A parallel path — `/webhooks/traycer-v2` — with a fresh secret. Both subscriptions now receive every event.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Deploy the handler for the new URL">
    Verify with the new secret there. Confirm real deliveries are arriving and verifying.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Move your traffic">
    Point processing at the new handler once you trust it.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Delete the old subscription">
    [Delete](/api-reference/webhooks/delete-subscription) the original. Nothing was missed at any point.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Rotate in place only when the secret is actually compromised — there, a gap in deliveries beats an attacker who can forge them.

## After rotating

<Steps>
  <Step title="Store the secret immediately">
    Straight into your secret manager. If you lose this response, your only recovery is another rotation, which loses more events.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Deploy it">
    Every instance verifying signatures needs the new value.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Confirm a real delivery verifies">
    Watch for the first `transaction.deposit` or `transaction.withdrawal` after the deploy. A spike in signature failures means the rollout did not land everywhere.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Errors

Both `400` with `validationErrors`:

| `message` | Cause |
| --- | --- |
| `subscriptionId must be a valid UUID` | Malformed identifier. |
| `Webhook subscription not found or does not belong to this organization` | No such subscription in your organization. |
